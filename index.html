OSCE Strategy & Tips

High-yield advice for performing under examination conditions.

  • Before you enter: Read the station brief twice. Note the exact task (history only, examination, full assessment, counselling) and the time limit.
  • Opening (WIPE): Wash hands, Introduce yourself (name + role), confirm Patient identity and preferred name, Explain what you are about to do and obtain consent.
  • History structure: Presenting complaint → History of presenting complaint (SOCRATES / OPQRST) → Past medical / surgical → Medications & allergies → Family history → Social (smoking, alcohol, occupation, living situation) → Systems review (targeted).
  • Examination: Always start with general inspection from the end of the bed. Work systematically (inspection → palpation → percussion → auscultation). Present findings clearly and confidently.
  • Red flags: Verbalise life-threatening differentials early (ACS, PE, aortic dissection, meningitis, sepsis, DKA, etc.).
  • Investigations: Request in a logical order (bedside → bloods → imaging → specialist). Justify each test briefly.
  • Management: ABCDE approach for the acutely unwell. Then definitive treatment, monitoring, escalation and safety-netting.
  • Communication: Speak clearly to both the patient and the examiner. Summarise findings at the end of the station.
  • Time management: Allocate time deliberately. Leave 1–2 minutes for summary and questions from the examiner.
  • Common pitfalls: Forgetting consent, missing red-flag questions, incomplete systems review, not examining relevant systems, vague differentials, unsafe management plans.
